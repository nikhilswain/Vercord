# Mosswild: A Place to Return

**Story, exploration, puzzles and rewards — proposal for review.**

This is a proposed direction, not a description of implemented gameplay. It expands
the existing Hollow Choir story without replacing its characters or ending. Item
effects, encounter rules and playtime estimates below are starting points for
playtesting. This task changes documentation only.

## 1. The recommendation

Make Mosswild **one connected story told through different regional experiences**.

The temple should be the dramatic turning point. The forest before it explains why
the rescue matters; the forest after it lets the player experience its consequences.
Each region also needs a small story worth finishing on its own.

The player's journey is:

> Reach the people stranded near the temple. Discover what went wrong. Free the
> keeper. Help the town and forest find a way to live beside each other again.

That gives exploration a purpose beyond increasing a counter. A broken crossing,
an abandoned garden and a missing bell become parts of the same history, while
offering different things to do.

**The emotional promise:** you arrive as a visitor and become someone whose return
people look forward to. The forest remains mysterious, but it gradually becomes a
place you know how to read.

The player is not secretly the chosen heir of the keeper. Their importance comes
from what they notice, repair and choose to help.

## 2. What already exists, and what this adds

The existing [Hollow Choir story](stories/hollow-choir.md) establishes:

- Vey and six choir members mistook a binding rite for a protective blessing.
- The forest keeper is imprisoned in a sleeping, humanlike form.
- The courtyard **Root Beast** seals the sanctuary entrance. Its defeat is required.
- The **Bound Warden** inside feeds on the trapped keeper. It is a separate creature.
- Releasing the sun and moon seals exposes the Warden; defeating it allows the rescue.
- The keeper returns to the forest. Vey promises to repair the paths without binding it.
- The traveler retrieves Mira's notes from the west reliquary and returns them to her.

Keep all of that. In particular, do not turn the keeper into the enemy, let a field
boss substitute for the courtyard boss, or move the notes out of the reliquary.

Implemented foundation (19 September 2026): the twelve-region server forest now
connects to the temple through the outer-approach arch in Rootbound Reach. The
journal continues from the three introductory discoveries into Mira’s courtyard,
the required guardian, both sanctuary seals, the Bound Warden, keeper rescue and
return of the notes. Explorers can reach it without those opening discoveries.
This is the existing Hollow Choir chapter; the broader regional puzzles, NPC arcs
and post-rescue consequences proposed below remain planned. Its existing places are described in [Mosswild](stories/mosswild.md)
and its current scope in [FOREST.md](FOREST.md).

**Proposed addition:** the old promise included ordinary acts of care: maintaining
waterways, keeping crossings safe, and leaving the keeper a way to come and go.
As people stopped maintaining the old routes, flooding and roots closed them.
Vey tried to solve that danger through a ritual. The binding made things worse.

This does not make the keeper cruel or secretly responsible for a punishment.
Neglected paths, frightened people and a predatory creature explain the crisis.

Mira already understands that the ritual is a binding. Do not pretend this is a
surprise she withholds until the finale. The wider mystery is **what the old promise
meant in practice, and how to restore it without repeating Vey's mistake**.

### Give the recurring people an arc

The following character details are proposed additions to the established cast:

- **Vey:** he learned his first songs at Old Ward's school. When the paths to his
  home began closing, singing was the way he knew to help. His fear made him cling
  to the ritual despite the harm. Afterward, show him doing ordinary repair work;
  an apology alone does not finish his story.
- **Mira:** she joined the original expedition and regrets trusting that a correct
  reading would keep everyone safe. Recovering her notes proves what happened;
  helping people apply them gives her a purpose beyond being right.
- **Juniper:** she notices the practical consequences others overlook: a blocked
  channel, missing pollinators, a camp without medicine. Her observations help
  turn an ancient promise into something the player can actually do.
- **Oren:** make him reliable about danger and retreat. His advice should accurately
  prepare the player for the dungeon, earning trust through gameplay.
- **Wren:** remember small things the player did and supply warmth between difficult
  stretches. When the traveler returns, Wren should recognize a friend. These story
  appearances do not require implementing a following, fighting NPC companion.

Give each person a short scene before and after their important change. Longer
history can remain optional. The player should care through encounters, not need
to read every inscription to understand someone's feelings.

## 3. The opening: a reason to enter the forest

Working opening scene, using Wren as a story contact in addition to the existing
social demonstration role:

> Wren sets a travel lantern on the ground. Its glass pulses with a long, uneven note.
>
> **Wren:** “Mira sent this back from the ruins. Listen. It keeps humming after I
> close it.”
>
> A nearby flower turns toward the sound.
>
> **Wren:** “She's safe at the southern camp. But the old supply crossing is gone,
> and the people inside the temple won't stop singing.”
>
> **Wren:** “Juniper was finding another way through Alder Run. Start at her camp.”

The pulsing lantern and a readable caption carry the clue even with sound off.
There is no real-time deadline and no implication that Mira dies while you explore.
The player immediately knows who needs help, where to begin and what is unusual.

The first 15–20 minutes should contain:

1. A brief conversation and a visible destination: Juniper's field camp.
2. One readable slime encounter, with room to avoid it and explore nearby trees.
3. The split oak's blue cloth teaching the player how safe routes are marked.
4. A small optional animal-track discovery with an immediate useful reward.
5. Juniper's explanation of the damaged crossing and a view of its mechanism.

Do not start with a board asking for twenty kills. Do not put the first meaningful
conversation several empty map crossings away.

## 4. The campaign in six chapters

### Chapter 1 — The road that stopped answering

**Place:** town and Mosswild Verge. **Target:** 20–25 minutes.

Meet Wren, follow the blue cloth and reach Juniper. Learn that several old routes
are failing at once. The leaning milestone's surviving word, “Choir,” connects this
ordinary-looking forest to the humming lantern.

**Player activity:** orientation, one encounter, tracking, first discovery.

**Payoff:** a safe camp, a clear lead toward Alder Run and someone who recognizes
you when you return. The map gains a lead, not every secret's exact coordinates.

### Chapter 2 — Water remembers the way

**Place:** Alder Run and Greater Fern Hollow. **Target:** 35–45 minutes.

Restore an old millrace crossing and reach the sunken gardens. Juniper discovers
that an apparently ornamental channel also supplied the healing terraces.

The same sustained note can be felt in the roots beside the water. The waterworks
are damaged; this is not an enchanted river demanding a tribute.

**Player activity:** a water-routing puzzle, a dangerous clearing with a quieter
approach, and a garden investigation. Hunting can supply optional food.

**Payoff:** a useful crossing, a garden starting to recover, a basic camp recipe,
and two onward leads: investigate Old Ward or follow the lamps through Lantern Wood.

### Chapter 3 — A shelter, not a summons

**Place:** Old Ward OR Lantern Wood, then Rootbound Reach. **Target:** 25–35 minutes.

Give the player a genuine route choice.

- **Old Ward:** the archive and keeper's threshold show that people once welcomed
  the keeper as a guest. Explore a settlement and recover its meaning.
- **Lantern Wood:** follow the abandoned pilgrim road. Its lamps guided travelers
  safely through the forest; they were never meant to lure something into a cage.

Either route supplies enough understanding to continue. Completing both adds
context and rewards, but is not required to unlock the temple.

At Rootbound Reach, find Mira and Oren's southern camp. Mira gives the existing
explanation of the binding. Oren establishes the courtyard boss requirement,
safe retreat and what happens if the player falls.

**Payoff:** the problem becomes personal and specific. The player knows the plan
before entering the dungeon. Establish a checkpoint here.

### Chapter 4 — The Hollow Choir

**Place:** Rootbound Temple courtyard and sanctuary. **Target:** 40–50 minutes.

Use the existing story sequence:

```text
Defeat courtyard Root Beast
  → enter sanctuary
  → release sun and moon seals, in either order
  → defeat Bound Warden
  → free keeper at altar
  → collect Mira's notes
  → return to Mira
```

Retain the actual boss gate even if someone arrives through a direct link or with
a party whose members have different progress.

Improve the seal presentation when implementing this campaign: rotating patterns,
visible links to the altar, a change in the chorus when each releases, and readable
hazard shutdown. A seal should visibly change the room when broken. It should not
feel like extinguishing an unrelated fire pot.

**Proposed hand-in reward:** Mira gives the traveler a repaired **Wayfarer's Lantern**
and one **Lastlight Seed**, once. The lantern is a practical tool she prepared;
the reward is not stolen power from the freed keeper.

Her existing closing dialogue remains valid. Add a follow-up after its completion:

> **Mira:** “We freed the keeper. That doesn't rebuild a crossing or clear a spring.”
>
> **Mira:** “These pages describe a welcome, renewed by the people who lived here.
> Vey thought the promise was something the forest owed us.”
>
> **Mira:** “Take the lantern to Moonmere. The old path there was meant to be found
> from both sides.”

This closes the rescue properly and introduces a new objective. Mira must never
return to asking the player to rescue an already-freed keeper or recover returned notes.

**This is the best stopping point for the first session.** It is a complete success,
with a reward, a safe camp and an understandable reason to return tomorrow.

### Chapter 5 — The promise we make back

**Place:** Moonmere and Elderheart. **Target:** 45–60 minutes.

Follow the old water channel through Old Ward to Moonmere, or explore the northern
loop before arriving there. The lantern reveals faded path markings at specific
old monuments. A reflection puzzle opens a secluded route to the homeward crossing.

Reach Elderheart's hollow circle. The keeper is free and has returned here; do not
reveal another hidden prison that makes the temple victory meaningless.

The remaining problem is tangible: the damaged watercourse is pushing through an
old passage, and cutting all its roots would collapse the structures they support.
The traveler, Vey and Juniper repair a way through without clearing the living grove.

**Finale activity:** apply what the forest taught you. Read the water marks, open a
spillway, use counterweights to raise an existing walkway, and turn the last guide
lamp toward the way out. These are nearby stages of one scene, not another circuit
of the entire forest to collect keys.

Let the keeper choose to cross the open passage. For the first time the player
hears an answer to the old note, followed by ordinary birds and moving water.

> **Vey:** “I thought a promise meant nothing could leave.”
>
> **Mira:** “Then let this one mean there will be a way back.”

The last action is opening a path, not killing a larger copy of the temple boss.
The Warden remains the main story's combat climax. Optional hunts provide further
combat challenges for players who want them.

### Chapter 6 — A place to return

**Place:** camp and town. **Target:** 10–15 minutes.

Return through an unlocked travel route. Wren recognizes the now-quiet lantern.
Vey begins work at a crossing instead of restarting his chant. Juniper's camp has
new sketches of the garden. The journal records what the player actually changed.

**Rewards:** the title **Trailkeeper**, a lantern appearance for the home camp,
and a choice of an existing weapon-family reward suitable for the player's progress.
Ember and Tide remain abilities; they are not the weapon choices.

The first restoration project can be chosen: help the choir rebuild a boardwalk,
or help Juniper recover the garden. Both remain available afterward. This gives
the player a preference to express without a permanently wrong ending.

Close the chapter before teasing another region. A new sound or a letter can hint
at the next journey, but the keeper does not disappear or get captured again.

## 5. Where the temple belongs, and how free exploration works

Add a clearly named **Rootbound Temple** entrance at **the outer approach in
Rootbound Reach**. It leads to the existing courtyard, then the sanctuary. Give
it a temple marker on the map and an accessible clearing, separate from town
channel buildings and ordinary region gates.

The suggested first route uses existing adjacent regions:

```text
Town → Mosswild Verge → Alder Run → Greater Fern Hollow
                                    ├→ Old Ward ────────┐
                                    └→ Lantern Wood ────┤
                                                       ↓
                                                 Rootbound Reach
                                                       ↓
                                          Temple courtyard → Sanctuary
```

After the temple, a direct story route is:

```text
Rootbound Reach → Old Ward → Moonmere → Elderheart
```

An optional northern exploration route is:

```text
Old Ward → Bracken Marsh → Stillwater Basin
                                  ↓
                             High Boughs → Foxglove Ridge
                                                ↓
                                            Moonmere → Elderheart
```

Keep the twelve-region network open. Story progress unlocks particular mechanisms,
conversations and the temple entrance; it does not put a mandatory boss toll at
every regional portal.

If someone reaches Moonmere or Elderheart early, they can explore, hunt, discover
the landmarks and find optional rewards. Inspecting an unfinished story mechanism
provides a concrete lead. The finale waits for the keeper's rescue and Moonmere's
route discovery; the whole region does not become an invisible wall.

The small demo remains a shorter introduction using the same temple story source.
Greater Fern Hollow in this campaign is not the small demo's Fern Hollow map.

## 7. Four puzzles worth building carefully

### A. Alder Run — The crossing that drank the garden

**Goal:** power the old crossing mechanism without flooding the lower garden channel.

**What the player sees:** an upstream diverter, a three-position sluice, a stalled
wheel mechanism and the existing flood marker. Water visibly responds to each control.

**Clues:** the middle notch on the marker carries a small wheel symbol; the upper
notch has flood damage. A visible side channel leads toward the garden. Inspecting
the wheel explains that it needs a steady flow rather than a full flood.

**Solution:** direct the upstream flow toward the millrace, then set the sluice to
the middle mark. The wheel turns, the crossing rises and overflow enters the garden
channel. Low flow cannot lift it; high flow submerges the mechanism and makes it stall.

**Why it works:** the player reads the environment, makes a prediction and sees a
result. Use designed water states, not a full fluid simulation.

**Failure:** harmless, reversible settings. Nobody is washed away, no item is consumed
and no mandatory enemy respawns. An ordinary ford keeps the wider region traversable.

**Hints:** “The wheel needs a steady flow” → “Compare its carving to the flood marks”
→ an explicit setting explanation if requested.

### B. Lantern Wood — Follow the guide's shadow

**Goal:** cross a short overgrown pilgrim passage to reach the next lamp post.

**What the player sees:** three forks in one small area. A hooded travel lamp can
be placed on either of two existing hooks at each fork. One placement casts an
arched shadow across the correct gap in the roots; the other casts a closed ring.

**Clue:** the first post has an intact carving of a traveler walking through the
open arch. At the Pilgrim's Table, the turned seat faces a gap rather than the center.

**Solution:** use the hook that produces an open arch and follow that gap. The first
fork teaches the rule clearly. Later forks change the viewing angle rather than
introducing unrelated codes. The passage rejoins the main route after three forks.

**Payoff:** a safer approach, an old traveler's cache and an image that prepares the
player for the temple's distinction between shelter and confinement.

**Failure:** the wrong branch loops back after a few steps. No teleport maze, forced
combat reset or need to remember ten directions. A basic lamp is provided here;
Mira's later reward is not required to reach Mira.

Use authored shadow sprites and limited attachment points. This does not require
a dynamic lighting engine. An inspectable carving also states the open-arch rule.

### C. Moonmere — The path seen from both sides

**Goal:** reach the sheltered crossing beyond the reflected arch.

**What the player sees:** the real arch is broken, while its reflection looks
complete. Three pale standing stones have footprints carved at their bases.
The lantern reveals matching marks on old stepping stones.

**Solution:** stand at each marked viewpoint. From that position, a reflected
branch aligns with a corresponding shoreline notch and identifies the next section
of the old stone path. Reading all three records the complete safe crossing.

The stones are real and become clearly outlined when found. The player is not
expected to walk blindly into water. Leaving and returning preserves discoveries.

**Earlier preparation:** Stillwater teaches the reflection idea, but Moonmere has
its own complete clues for players who skipped Stillwater.

**Payoff:** the homeward route and a brief depiction of the old welcome: an open
passage, people tending its sides, and the keeper free to leave. Keep this scene
short enough to be understood without opening several journal pages.

**Accessibility:** offer a readable description of each alignment and a clear path
highlight. Do not require a particular screen zoom, hearing, real moon phase or
waiting for night in the actual world.

### D. Elderheart — Leave a way through

**Goal:** make one shared passage safe without destroying the roots holding it up.

**Preparation:** a nearby overlook shows the spillway, broken walkway and blocked
exit in one view. Juniper points out the living roots supporting the stonework.

**Solution:** divert water into the existing spillway, use the counterweights to
raise the walkway, then turn the guide lamp toward the open exit. Each action makes
the next physically understandable. Vey operates the second crank when needed;
a latch makes the same mechanism work for a solo player.

**Payoff:** the keeper crosses by choice, the passage stays usable and Vey begins
fulfilling the promise he made in the temple.

This is a calm, active ending after the Warden. Do not add a timed escort bar or
surprise fight while a long conversation prevents the player from acting.

## 9. Items: useful choices, not a pile of colored duplicates

**September 20 design revision:** use [Alchemy and Provisions](ALCHEMY_AND_PROVISIONS.md)
for the proposed item economy, recipes, food/Vitality rules, stations and build order.
It replaces the older healing-wrap and cloth progression below. The older item
table is retained as campaign history, not an additional implementation backlog.
The temple sequence and the later downed/rescue design remain separate from that
recipe revision. The Hearthstone already provides reusable return-to-town travel;
do not add the older Wayhome Knot as a duplicate travel requirement.

Separate inventory by purpose:

- **Food:** meals and immediate recovery. Raw meat and raw fowl belong here but
  require cooking before eating.
- **Supplies:** recovery preparations, rescue consumables and travel consumables.
- **Materials:** ingredients with an actual recipe or announced use.
- **Tools:** permanent exploration utilities, such as the lantern.
- **Weapons:** the existing sword, axe, spear and staff families.
- **Quest items and keepsakes:** protected story objects and optional collections.

These categories expand the current prototype. They do not imply equipment armor,
an accessory slot system, elemental resistances or a complete crafting economy
already exist. Preserve the separation between weapons and Ember/Tide abilities.

Use common, uncommon and rare for availability. Use **unique** for named story
rewards, not another damage tier. A rare utility can stay useful after leveling.

### The ten-second revival idea

Recommended interpretation: **the player can use a Lastlight Seed within ten
seconds of reaching zero health**. It does not rewind the whole world by ten seconds.

Starting rules to test:

1. At zero HP, enter a readable ten-second downed state. Offer **Use Lastlight Seed**
   when available, or **Return to camp**. A solo player does not need a party to use it.
2. Using a seed restores 30% maximum HP where the player fell. It gives roughly two
   seconds of recovery protection, canceled by attacking, so the player can move
   out of the hit that downed them.
3. A nearby party member can instead complete a four-second rescue action for 20%
   HP without an item. The ribbon shortens this to two seconds and restores 40% HP;
   it is consumed from the rescuer's inventory. Rescuers remain vulnerable.
4. For an initial simple rule, the rescue must complete within the downed window.
   An interrupted action or late request does not consume an item. Make remaining
   time clear to both players; validate the window under realistic latency.
5. A player can be revived **once per encounter attempt**, regardless of method.
   A later knockdown in that same attempt sends them to camp. An encounter ends
   when its combatants are defeated or the group fully retreats and it resets;
   stepping across an arbitrary boundary does not refresh the allowance.
6. Expiry sends the player to the last safe camp. Keep earned items, discoveries,
   unlocked shortcuts and completed story steps. No corpse run for Mira's notes.
7. Reserve and consume the chosen item only on successful revival. Two allies,
   duplicated inputs or a reconnect must not spend multiple items or revive twice.

Boss health may reset after a full retreat or party wipe. Already released temple
seals and completed story facts stay completed. A single downed party member does
not reset the encounter for everyone still fighting.

This gives two rescue items different purposes: a seed lets you save yourself; a
ribbon helps you reach and save someone else under pressure. We do not need five
different revival flowers that only increase the percentage slightly.

Make the ten-second default adjustable for solo accessibility; agree one window
for a shared encounter rather than letting each client invent its own timer. The
exact duration and recovery values are proposals, not final balance.

### Rarity without grinding away motivation

- The main story guarantees one seed. At least two optional secrets give predictable
  opportunities to earn additional seeds. A player can learn where these come from.
- Rare items improve a risky expedition; no story gate requires spending one.
- Story and secret rewards are granted per eligible player, once. A friend arriving
  first does not empty the campaign's only supply for everyone else.
- Keep unique quest objects out of selling, dropping and crafting consumption.
- Avoid a weight system and repeated inventory sorting in the first campaign.
- After the one-time sources are spent, any renewable rare source needs a designed
  encounter/reset rule. Current cleared forest encounters do not yet form a timed
  respawn economy. Do not promise limitless seed farming or reroll rewards on refresh.

## 10. Hunting should support the journey

Keep abundant slimes, animals in the undergrowth and several interesting predators.
Give their placement meaning:

- Slimes gather around damp roots, fallen fruit and abandoned water channels.
- Skirmishers control sightlines where there is cover to approach them.
- Brutes occupy a few noticeable clearings rather than every intersection.
- Two-headed flower creatures have recognizable hunting grounds and clear warning
  signs. They can guard optional caches; they are not mandatory tolls between regions.
- Peaceful animal pockets, camps and puzzle spaces provide a change of pace.

The current large population is a starting point, not a requirement to fight
hundreds of enemies per chapter. Protect story interactions and puzzle controls
from random aggro. A creature should not spawn inside the clue someone is reading.

Offer three optional authored hunts after their regions are introduced: a brute
near an old supply route, a flower creature in a garden clearing, and a predator
plant beside a ruined watercourse. Each needs a distinct arena and a guaranteed
first-clear reward. Use current enemy families before commissioning new ones.

Hunting gives food and preparation materials; puzzles and stories give tools,
routes and unique rewards. Exploration should also award enough progression that
a player who investigates and avoids optional hunts can complete the main story.

The keeper's release should visibly quiet the ritual-affected places, without
turning every predator friendly or removing all future combat from the forest.

## 11. Keeping the player motivated

At any point, give the player three scales of purpose:

| Scale             | Example                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Right now         | “Get the crossing moving so you can reach the garden.”                  |
| This session      | “Reach Mira and free the keeper.”                                       |
| The wider journey | “Find out how the town can belong here without imprisoning the forest.” |

Story guidance is optional. Free exploration starts with no story trail and no
permanent objective tracker. Current objectives, the next lead, optional discoveries
and story history belong in a **Journey** screen available from **Esc → Journey**
and a **Journey** entry in Map. Both entry points open the same progress view.

### Player choice: follow the story or explore freely

**User direction, 19 September 2026.** The shared Journey screen and optional guidance
are implemented; see [JOURNEY.md](JOURNEY.md). The expanded forest currently connects
three existing discoveries into an opening lead: Juniper's field camp, the leaning
milestone, and Alder Run's millrace, then continues into the connected Rootbound
Temple chapter. The same Hollow Choir story facts drive both the server expedition
and original demo journal. The full regional NPC chains, puzzles and additional
rewards below remain planned. The following requirements apply as the campaign grows.

- **Follow story** opens the current chapter, a short recap, what the player has
  accomplished, and the next actionable objective with its named location. Choosing
  this never starts navigation, moves the camera, or moves the character.
- **Explore freely** closes the journal and lets the player continue exploring.
  Story progress remains saved. Players can return to the story whenever they want;
  neither choice creates a separate save, locks regions, or resets rewards.
- The journal offers **Show on map** to locate the known objective, and a separate
  **Guide me** action to explicitly start the golden trail. Reading a quest,
  entering a region, or speaking to an NPC never starts a trail automatically.
- An optional **Pin objective** action can show one compact HUD reminder. It is
  separate from trail navigation and can be hidden again. Without that choice,
  "what should I do next?" remains available in Journey without occupying gameplay.
- Reaching the destination stops its trail. Completing a step updates the journal;
  it does not automatically guide the player to the following step. Choosing free
  exploration removes story guidance while retaining story progress.
- Players can discover and complete eligible story actions through exploration.
  Opening or tracking a quest is not a prerequisite for its normal gameplay triggers.
  NPC dialogue, landmarks and world changes must communicate the story without a trail.
- Esc closes the current foreground panel first. From normal gameplay it opens the
  menu, where Journey is easy to find. Map and the touch menu provide the same access.

Example presentation inside Journey:

```text
A Voice Beyond the Trees
Chapter 1 · The road that stopped answering

So far: Wren shared Mira's message from the ruins.
Next: Find Juniper's field camp in Mosswild Verge.

[ Show on map ]   [ Guide me ]
[ Pin objective ]   [ Explore freely ]
```

Only show objectives backed by implemented story content. Do not send players to
an unimplemented puzzle or imply that the large forest already connects to the temple.

A useful journal entry reads:

> **The old crossing**
>
> Juniper needs a safe route to the garden. The wheel is intact, but the sluice is
> set above the old flood mark.
>
> **Next:** inspect the flood marker beside the millrace in Alder Run.
>
> **Expected reward:** a safe crossing and Juniper's camp recipe.

Avoid “Explore Alder Run: 3/8” as the main reason to continue. Discovery counts may
exist for completionists, but should not replace an understandable task.

Provisional pacing targets:

- Within the first five minutes, know a person's name, a problem and a destination.
- Every 5–10 minutes on the main route, encounter a new decision, clue, useful
  resource opportunity or visible change. This does not mean a notification every minute.
- Every 20–35 minutes, complete something with a payoff: a crossing, a conversation,
  a recipe, a secret or a new view that changes the player's understanding.
- After a difficult encounter, allow a quiet stretch and a safe place to stop.
- Let optional clues create curiosity: a lit shelter across water, a raven carrying
  metal, a path on an old drawing that the current map does not show.
- Returning to familiar places must reveal remembered actions, not reset dialogue.

Avoid compulsory daily chores, real-time waits, missable rewards for logging in
at a certain hour, or low-probability drops required for the next chapter.

### Playtime and the two-session target

The chapter budgets total about **175–230 minutes** for a focused story route.
Optional routes, the beekeeper story, secrets and hunts should contribute another
**90–120 minutes** for an interested explorer: roughly **4.5–6 hours in total**.

These are content budgets to test, not measured playtime. A confident replay can
be faster. Completion of every discovery site is outside that target.

**First session:** introduction, crossings, a chosen approach and temple rescue.
Stop after Mira's hand-in, with a clear next lead.

**Second session:** follow the northern discoveries if desired, solve Moonmere,
finish Elderheart and return home. Resolve remaining side stories afterward.

If the prototype is too short, add decisions, encounters and character payoffs.
Do not increase walking distances or enemy health to manufacture six hours.

## 12. Solo, parties and reliable progress

The campaign must work alone. Friends should make exploration richer rather than
being mandatory keys for pressure plates or doors.

- Pair mechanisms with latches, timed sequences or a nearby story character so a
  solo player can operate them. Co-op can be convenient without being required.
- Party members in the same expedition can share puzzle actions and encounter
  progress. Each keeps their own reward claims and completion history.
- Credit active participants together, including a player downed during the final
  boss hit. Someone elsewhere in the world should not silently skip the campaign.
- Let a late joiner see completed mechanisms and a short journal recap. Do not
  automatically mark their personal campaign finished merely for joining a party.
- A newcomer can assist friends on a later chapter, then complete missing personal
  prerequisites through their own expedition or replay. Never require the whole
  public server to reset a story for one new traveler.
- The sanctuary checks the courtyard clear for that expedition. A party invite,
  teleport, reload or another player's unrelated field kill cannot substitute for it.
- After leaving or reconnecting, preserve claimed rewards, opened routes and story
  facts. Do not depend only on the dialogue currently displayed.
- Main story dialogue should pause or protect the participating encounter where
  appropriate. It should not freeze the whole server or expose a reader to surprise hits.

Treat substantial story changes as expedition state with personal completion,
while maintaining the server's shared forest geography. A newcomer's unfinished
quest must not put a wall across a veteran's ordinary regional route. Shared town
construction votes and permanent player-controlled terrain are separate future systems.

Track a few explicit facts rather than interpreting inventory absence as progress:

```text
crossing_restored
approach_understood
courtyard_root_beast_defeated
moon_seal_released + sun_seal_released
bound_warden_defeated
keeper_freed
mira_notes_recovered → mira_notes_returned
mira_reward_claimed
moonmere_crossing_read
elderheart_passage_restored
campaign_completed
```

**Current limitation:** forest combat, inventory and journey progress are local to
the browser. The temple story's current demo persistence is more limited still.
These multiplayer and durable reward guarantees are requirements for future work,
not capabilities this document adds. Reliable shared combat, reward claims and
revival validation need server authority before multiplayer progression can be trusted.

Follow the existing [backend architecture direction](BACKEND_ARCHITECTURE.md) when
that implementation begins. Keep content and game rules separate from storage.
This proposal does not start a database migration or change the PostgreSQL plan.

## 13. Build order: finish one satisfying journey before filling everything

### First playable slice: the crossing and the camp

Target **30–45 minutes**, in parts of Verge and Alder Run:

1. Wren's opening and Juniper's practical problem.
2. One authored crossing puzzle, one optional tracking secret and two deliberately
   placed encounters with space for avoidance and recovery.
3. A camp checkpoint, an existing-herb supply and one simple meal.
4. Remembered dialogue, a visible repaired crossing and an explicit next lead.
5. A separate, clearly designated test encounter for the proposed revival behavior.

No rare revival drop is required to finish this slice. It tests whether players
care about the journey before the full campaign is authored.

### Then connect and complete the temple journey

Add the garden lead, one finished approach through Old Ward or Lantern Wood,
Rootbound Reach's camp and the actual temple connection. Reuse the existing Hollow
Choir story data. Complete the Mira hand-in, lantern reward and checkpoint recovery.

Only after that works, add the alternate approach. Do not build two half-finished
routes before either provides a complete experience.

### Then earn the second session

Build Moonmere's puzzle and Elderheart's ending. Add the northern side story,
eight secrets and three optional hunts in a deliberate order. Connect their clues
to existing landmarks instead of scattering unrelated loot containers.

Keep the production scope bounded:

- Six main chapters, with the temple already providing one chapter's story base.
- Four reusable puzzle concepts, each with authored local arrangements.
- One substantial optional NPC story, eight secrets and three optional hunts.
- Existing main cast, plus one proposed new resident, Sella.
- Existing enemies and licensed forest art; new effects and props should follow
  the same pixel-art style and the project's free-only asset preference.
- A small useful item set, expanded as its actual recipes and effects are built.

Defer a full economy, trading, durability, armor sets, procedural quest generation,
daily missions, large branching endings and a second continent. None is needed to
prove this chapter is enjoyable.

Authored puzzles also need valid placement inside the generated forest: correct
line of sight, accessible controls, readable props and safe space around clues.
Reserve these areas in the layout and test multiple world seeds. Never let a tree,
random enemy or water tile make the intended solution impossible.

## 14. How we decide whether this is working

During a first playtest, watch behavior before explaining anything:

- Can the player explain why they entered the forest and where they are going next?
- Do they notice the first optional clue without an arrow pointing directly at it?
- Can they solve a puzzle from its world clues, with hints available if needed?
- Does the temple hand-in feel like a completed story rather than dialogue ending?
- Can they explain the difference between the keeper, the Root Beast and the Warden?
- Do they stop at a camp knowing what they want to do next session?
- Do rewards change their decisions, or merely add more objects to a list?
- Does a defeat cost some tension and preparation without erasing an evening's progress?
- Can a solo player finish, and can friends help without broken quest ownership?
- Is most of the session playing, observing and choosing, rather than walking through
  repeated terrain or dismissing identical enemy groups?

If players cannot explain their next goal, improve the lead. If they cannot explain
why they care, improve the character and payoff. Extra map area will not solve either.

## 15. Decisions to carry into implementation

Recommended defaults from this proposal:

1. **Connected main story, distinct regional experiences.**
2. **Rootbound Reach hosts the real temple approach.**
3. **The existing rescue remains intact; Elderheart provides its consequence and ending.**
4. **The forest network stays open; specific story mechanisms have clear prerequisites.**
5. **Rare rewards have understandable sources and never gate the main story.**
6. **Lastlight Seed is a ten-second self-rescue opportunity, with bounded encounter use.**
7. **Restoration, relationships and useful tools matter alongside hunting and weapons.**
8. **Build and playtest the small complete slice first.**
9. **Story or free exploration is the player's choice. Journey in Esc/Map holds
   progress and next steps; golden trails and HUD objective reminders require an
   explicit player action.**

The details most worth testing early are movement time between authored events,
puzzle readability, the revival window, and whether the quiet ending feels earned
after the temple fight. Those should shape the final numbers and scope.
